United States Constitutional Amendments

Bill of Rights (Amendments 1–10)

#DescriptionDate RatifiedSummary
1Freedoms of Religion, Speech, Press, Assembly, and Petition1791-12-15Prohibits Congress from making laws respecting an establishment of religion, impeding free exercise, abridging freedom of speech or press, interfering with assembly, or prohibiting petition for redress of grievances.
2Right to Bear Arms1791-12-15Protects the right of the people to keep and bear arms.
3Quartering of Soldiers1791-12-15Prohibits forced quartering of soldiers in private homes during peacetime without consent.
4Search and Seizure1791-12-15Protects against unreasonable searches and seizures; requires judicially sanctioned warrants supported by probable cause.
5Rights in Criminal Cases1791-12-15Guarantees grand jury, forbids double jeopardy, protects against self-incrimination; requires due process and just compensation for takings.
6Right to a Fair Trial1791-12-15Guarantees public trial without unnecessary delay, right to lawyer, impartial jury, and right to know accusers and charges.
7Rights in Civil Cases1791-12-15Guarantees jury trial in certain civil cases; inhibits courts from overturning jury’s findings of fact.
8Bail, Fines, and Punishment1791-12-15Prohibits excessive bail and fines, and cruel and unusual punishment.
9Rights Retained by the People1791-12-15Enumeration of specific rights does not deny other rights retained by the people.
10Powers Retained by the States and the People1791-12-15Federal government possesses only powers delegated by the Constitution; all else reserved to states or people.
