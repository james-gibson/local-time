Costco Wholesale

Overview

Costco Wholesale Corporation is an American multinational warehouse club, founded in Seattle in 1983 by James Sinegal and Jeffrey Brotman. Members pay an annual fee for the right to shop in a bare industrial building, buying goods by the pallet at near-wholesale prices. The model — membership, bulk, thin margins, a strong house brand — made Costco one of the defining institutions of American consumer capitalism.

It appears in this knowledge base because a work of fiction treats it as more than a shop. See Reinterpretation, below.

History

The warehouse-club formation (1983-1993)

The first Costco warehouse opened in Seattle on 15 September 1983. The warehouse-club format — membership fees and bulk purchasing — was being pioneered at the same time by The Price Company (“Price Club”), which had opened in San Diego in 1976. Through the late 1980s the two chains grew in parallel and in direct competition.

Scale and private label (1993-)

In 1993 Costco and Price Club merged, forming PriceCostco; the merged company was renamed Costco in 1997. The period that followed is the one that fixed the company’s image: national scale, and Kirkland Signature, the house brand introduced in the mid-1990s that made the store’s own name a byword for generically good and cheap. That image — the vast warehouse, the membership card, the store brand on everything — is the raw material a later satire would use.

Reinterpretation

Idiocracy (2006) reimagines Costco as the state itself: in the film’s 2505, public functions are performed inside a Costco — it is the courthouse, its “law school” awards a degree in a day, and the store’s branding stands in for government. The film is not making a claim about the real company; the Idiocracy record carries the relation as a PARODIES edge, with the evidence attached, so the parody is recorded rather than laundered into a statement about Costco.

This is also why the company has its own universe rather than being a bare string in another record: a substantial real referent gets a real timeline, and fiction that uses it points at that timeline.
